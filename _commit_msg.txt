Fix why the choice questions barely improved: private readout rows per answer

Diagnosis (all measured, artifacts/REPORT_V8_CN.md):
- the slot head picked a row by the option's position, so training a 2-4 option task
  rewrote the rows a 10-15 option task reads (clinc150_top15 0.893 -> 0.733,
  hwu64_top15 0.613 -> 0.467, intentgrasp 0.350 -> 0.225 while the trained tasks rose)
- choice held only 14.7% of the gradient (goemotions alone gave 3360 decisions against
  ~120 for a choice task)
- the intent test sets were a label-sorted prefix: 400 items covered 40 of 77 classes
- decision states have norm ~157 against 0.74 for the pretrained label rows, so one step
  at lr 1e-3 moved a fresh row a fifth of its useful length (mean training loss 33.0
  against 5.01 for a uniform head)

Fixes:
- readout rows are private per (question, label space, answer); an answer the head never
  saw still reads its pretrained letter row, so unseen label spaces keep zero-shot
  behaviour exactly (tests/test_rlcd_tiny.py)
- scripts/build_extra_splits.py adds the missing training splits (MMLU-Pro from its
  unused test rows, CLINC150/HWU64 full-space and 15-intent subsets, BANKING77) and
  refuses to write a split whose answers its test file would not read
- normalize._iter_limited spreads the cap over the whole file instead of taking a prefix
- trained rows are projected back to the reserved rows' norm (--row-norm-cap), SGD is the
  default (Adam's per-coordinate scaling collapses a wide label space onto one direction),
  gradient clipping is off (it scaled a norm-60 gradient down 60x)
- train.py --prototype-init adds closed-form rows (the mean state of each answer) for
  label spaces wider than the 26 reserved rows, and scripts/compose_wide_rows.py joins
  them to the shared rows; the shipped head is that composition

Full benchmark (33 test splits, one run): bool 0.794, choice 0.671 over 18 tasks
(0.700 over the 15 the pretrained readout can answer), score 0.737, overall 0.713
against Laya 0.424. BANKING77 0.495, CLINC150 0.542, HWU64 0.542 (were 0.100/0.077/0.225,
on their old test files).
